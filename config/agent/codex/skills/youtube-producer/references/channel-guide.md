# MrDemonWolf YouTube channel guide

This compact guide travels with the skill so it works after upload to ChatGPT Web, even without access to Nathanial's Mac or this repository.

## Channel identity and voice

- Creator: Nathanial Henniges, a software engineer, host, and blue-wolf creator.
- Write as Nathanial in first person. Keep it warm, direct, practical, and lightly playful; use natural spoken language rather than polished ad copy.
- Channel identity: approachable software, web, creator workflow, and community content through the MrDemonWolf persona.
- Keep MrDemonWolf, company/client work, Kommit, and other projects distinct unless Nathanial connects them in the specific episode.
- Never expose private client or unreleased work. Discuss the public problem, process, or lesson and ask before using uncertain private details.

## Visual rules

- Follow the current brand guide in the MrDemonWolf website repo when available. Current core colors are Brand Navy `#0A1633` and Sky Blue `#3AAEE3`, with white and Wolf Smoke `#0D2A56` as supporting colors. The older YouTube pack uses `#091533` and `#0FACED`; use those only when Nathanial asks for that legacy look.
- The bundled approved blue-wolf artwork is available in the skill. Keep the complete artwork intact: preserve proportions, colors, and expression; do not crop, rotate, recolor, redraw, filter, outline, add accessories, or distort it. Scale uniformly and give it clear space on a plain, contrasting background. Never substitute a generic AI wolf or add extra characters.
- Use user-provided screenshots/photos as genuine source material. Keep UI and text authentic; don't invent screens, data, logos, results, or labels. If details are private, crop or blur only with direction.
- Make thumbnails look art-directed: one clear focal subject, real episode imagery, real lighting or a simple graphic background, strong but controlled contrast, and space for 2–4 words added in an editor.
- Avoid stock-looking AI gradients, random icons, excessive glow, cluttered collages, fake interface screenshots, misspelled AI text, and generic reaction poses unrelated to the story.
- Make choices materially different (for example: screenshot-led, character-led, or object-led), not the same layout with color changes.
- Use Montserrat for bold headline text added in an editor, Roboto for readable supporting copy, and JetBrains Mono only for technical labels. Keep wording personal, concrete, and welcoming; write as Nathanial (“I build…”), not as a company press release.
- Regular video: create up to three separate image candidates in 16:9. Shorts: offer three vertical 9:16 cover ideas, then compose the selected candidate; include a supplied photo/screenshot/frame when Nathanial wants one. Keep the vertical composition readable in the center safe area and do not assume thumbnail A/B testing is available for Shorts.
- Keep headline wording outside the generated image. Give the exact headline separately for Canva, Affinity, or another editor.

## Output sizes in the local publishing kit

- Regular video thumbnail: 3840 × 2160, 16:9.
- Shorts vertical cover: 2160 × 3840, 9:16.

Current YouTube help says custom Shorts thumbnails can be uploaded through YouTube Studio on desktop for eligible, verified accounts, with 9:16 recommended. Check current account/platform requirements before promising the upload path.

## Hashtag-first metadata

Use three relevant hashtags at the end of the description; keep the title clear without hashtags by default. Do not provide a separate comma-separated tags-field list unless asked. The YouTube tags field still exists but is a low-priority optional control, not a required step in Nathanial’s workflow. Natural keyword phrases are writing guidance, not a description keyword dump.

Keep YouTube hashtags inside the final description only. Do not add a separate Hashtags heading or duplicate copy block in Chat or Notion; platform-specific social captions may retain their own relevant hashtags.
